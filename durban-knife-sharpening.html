The case for sharpening

Why bother sharpening?

A blunt edge is more dangerous, more wasteful and more expensive than a sharp one. Here's the actual reasoning.

Force, not finesse

A dull blade needs pressure to cut. Pressure is what slips — and a slipping blade is how most kitchen injuries happen. A sharp edge does the work for you, with far less force behind it.

Clean cuts, better food

A honed edge slices through fibres instead of crushing them. Vegetables stay crisp, meat holds its texture, and fish fillets come apart clean instead of tearing.

Cheaper than a new knife

A decent knife costs upwards of R1,500. Keeping its edge costs a fraction of that every few months — for years more use out of the same blade.
